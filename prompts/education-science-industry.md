# Education, Science, and Industrial Process Prompts for Grok Imagine 1.5

These prompts make every teaching claim observable on screen. For factual or safety-critical publication, review the generated result with a qualified subject-matter expert before use.

## 1. Newton's Cradle — one-variable physics demonstration

**Mode:** Image-to-video recommended · **Duration:** 8s · **Aspect ratio:** 16:9 · **Audio:** collision foley + concise narration

```text
Animate the supplied laboratory image of a five-ball Newton's cradle. Preserve exactly five identical steel balls, equal string lengths, rigid rectangular frame, white tabletop, fixed side camera, and neutral side light.

0–2s — an adult hand enters slowly from the left, lifts only the leftmost ball to a 35-degree angle, pauses, and releases without pushing.
2–5s — the ball swings down and strikes the stationary group. The rightmost ball rises to a similar but slightly lower angle while the three middle balls remain visually close together. Show one clear return cycle at normal speed.
5–8s — amplitude decreases naturally; camera remains fixed while an off-screen narrator says, "Momentum passes through the touching balls; friction slowly removes energy from the motion."

PHYSICS: Strings stay taut, frame remains grounded, collisions occur at the center line, no ball gains height, and damping is gradual.

AUDIO: Finger release, crisp but realistic metal contacts, faint room tone, exact narration. No ticking soundtrack or music.

AVOID: six balls, floating strings, amplified swing, sparks, slow-motion distortion, formula text, logo, subtitles, watermark.
```

## 2. Ceramic Disk Test Cell — robotic quality-control cycle

**Mode:** Image-to-video recommended · **Duration:** 12s · **Aspect ratio:** 16:9 · **Audio:** industrial foley

```text
Animate the supplied pilot-lab reference. Preserve the exact six-axis robot, transparent safety enclosure, circular ceramic disk, metal test fixture, amber status beacon, fictional adult engineer, blue lab coat, safety glasses, gloves, and camera viewpoint.

0–3s — robot approaches at controlled speed with the ceramic disk held level in a vacuum gripper. Engineer remains outside the closed enclosure and watches.
3–7s — robot lowers the disk vertically into the circular fixture until contact; a mechanical clamp closes around the edge, then the gripper releases. No human reaches inside.
7–10s — test head descends a short measured distance and applies compression; one physical analog gauge needle moves smoothly into a green-colored band without readable numbers.
10–12s — test head rises, amber beacon turns steady green, and the engineer records the result on a blank clipboard.

CAMERA: Locked industrial-documentary composition with one modest focus pull from robot tool to analog gauge.

PHYSICS & SAFETY: Robot joints follow fixed axes, disk stays rigid, enclosure remains closed, tools never intersect, all human contact stays outside the cell.

AUDIO: Servo hum, clamp click, short pneumatic hiss, test press load, room ventilation. No narration or music.

AVOID: unsafe access, extra robot joints, cracked disk, flying parts, invented digital UI, readable data, brand, watermark.
```

## 3. Coffee Roasting State Change — process documentary

**Mode:** Text-to-video · **Duration:** 15s · **Aspect ratio:** 16:9 · **Audio:** workshop ambience + narration

```text
Create a 15-second factual process documentary in a small fictional coffee-roasting workshop. One adult roaster wears a dark apron and heat-safe gloves. Use one compact unbranded drum roaster with a round sampling trier and cooling tray.

0–4s — macro view of pale green coffee beans entering the metal drum; roaster closes the hopper and starts rotation. Narrator: "Green beans begin dense and grassy."
4–8s — match cut to the sampling trier opening after time has passed; beans are now light brown, slightly larger, and release visible steam. Roaster withdraws a small sample. Narrator: "Heat drives off moisture and builds new aromas."
8–12s — beans reach an even medium brown; one restrained first-crack sound occurs inside the drum, not an explosion. Roaster checks color and releases the batch.
12–15s — beans pour into the perforated cooling tray and move under rotating arms while air pulls steam downward. Narrator: "Fast cooling stops the roast at the chosen point."

CAMERA: Four clear documentary shots connected by matching circular forms; no claim that the full roast happens in real time.

PHYSICS: Bean color and size progress only forward, smoke remains minimal, hot metal is handled safely, cooling arms maintain contact above the beans.

AUDIO: Beans, drum motor, restrained crack, cooling fan, exact neutral narration. No music.

AVOID: fire, dramatic smoke, instant color morph inside one shot, bare-hand contact, branded bags, text labels, watermark.
```

## 4. Why Leaves Change Color — tabletop science explainer

**Mode:** Text-to-video · **Duration:** 13s · **Aspect ratio:** 9:16 · **Audio:** presenter explanation

```text
Create a 13-second vertical science explainer with one fictional adult educator at a clean tabletop. Three real leaves lie in a row: green, yellow, and red. Behind them is a blank neutral board with no text.

0–4s — medium shot. Educator holds the green leaf and says, "Leaves look green because chlorophyll reflects green light." They rotate the leaf once under a small white lamp.
4–8s — top-down table view. Educator places the green leaf back and points, in order, to green, yellow, then red while saying, "As days shorten, chlorophyll breaks down and other pigments become visible."
8–13s — macro view of the three leaf surfaces under the same light; educator adds off screen, "Weather and species affect the final color." A gentle fan moves only the leaf tips.

CAMERA: Three educational shots, accurate hand-to-object contact, no animated molecules or unsupported diagrams.

AUDIO: Exact calm explanation, subtle paper and leaf handling, quiet room. No music.

ACCURACY: Present this as a simplified overview, not a complete biochemical mechanism.

AVOID: instant color-changing leaf, fabricated formulas, child presenter, misleading certainty, on-screen words, logos, captions, watermark.
```

## 5. Transparent Water Pump — functional cutaway animation

**Mode:** Text-to-video · **Duration:** 10s · **Aspect ratio:** 16:9 · **Audio:** mechanical foley + narration

```text
Create a 10-second high-clarity 3D educational cutaway of a simple centrifugal water pump. The pump has one inlet pipe on the left, a rotating impeller inside a transparent casing, and one outlet pipe at the upper right. Use clean generic geometry with no brand or text.

0–3s — exterior three-quarter view. Clear water begins entering slowly through the left pipe while the impeller is still. Narrator: "Water enters near the center of the impeller."
3–7s — impeller accelerates clockwise to a stable moderate speed. Water follows visible curved paths outward through the casing and exits the upper-right pipe. Narrator: "Rotation adds energy and pushes the flow outward."
7–10s — camera performs a small 20-degree arc while flow remains steady; transparent casing briefly becomes half-cutaway to reveal the continuous path. Narrator: "The casing guides that flow toward the outlet."

PHYSICS: Impeller remains on its shaft, water volume is conserved, no cavitation, no reverse flow, and the outlet stream begins only after rotation.

AUDIO: Electric motor ramp, smooth water flow, exact narration. No dramatic music.

AVOID: incorrect extra inlet, changing blade count, floating components, arrows or labels, explosive pressure, logo, watermark.
```
