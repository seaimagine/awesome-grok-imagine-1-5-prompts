## 1. The Last Tram — restrained dialogue drama

**Mode:** Text-to-video · **Duration:** 10s · **Aspect ratio:** 16:9 · **Audio:** dialogue + rain + tram foley

```text
Create a 10-second photorealistic contemporary drama, 16:9.

SUBJECT LOCK: Two fictional adults in their early thirties. Passenger A wears a charcoal wool coat and sits on the left; Passenger B wears a dark navy jacket and sits on the right. Keep their faces, clothing, seat positions, and screen direction unchanged.

SCENE: The nearly empty final tram after midnight. Rain beads on the windows. Warm tungsten cabin lights contrast with cool blue city reflections.

TIMELINE: 0–3s — symmetrical wide shot down the aisle as the tram gently sways; both passengers notice each other through the window reflection. 3–7s — slow push toward Passenger A; their guarded expression softens, and they quietly say, "Long night?" 7–10s — rack focus to Passenger B, who gives a tired half-smile and nods while the tram passes under a brighter streetlight; hold the final glance for one second.

CAMERA: Stable 40mm cinema-lens feel, one continuous slow push, shallow but controlled depth of field, layered glass reflections.

MOTION & PHYSICS: Subtle body sway follows the tram, coat fabric responds with weight, raindrops streak downward, reflections move consistently with passing lights.

AUDIO: Soft wheel rumble, rain on glass, distant signal bell. Dialogue is intimate and naturally lip-synced. No music.

AVOID: melodramatic acting, face drift, seat changes, extra passengers appearing, jump cuts, subtitles, logos, text, watermark.
```

## 2. The Kiln Warning — tactile workshop thriller

**Mode:** Image-to-video recommended · **Duration:** 8s · **Aspect ratio:** 16:9 · **Audio:** workshop ambience

```text
Animate the supplied pottery-studio image into an 8-second grounded suspense scene. Preserve the exact artist, apron, workbench, clay vessel, kiln geometry, window placement, and warm-cool lighting contrast.

0–2s — macro view of wet fingers centering a clay bowl on a spinning wheel; the clay turns smoothly without changing volume. 2–5s — a low metallic knock comes from the closed kiln behind the artist; the wheel slows as their hands freeze and their eyes shift toward the sound. 5–8s — the camera makes a restrained shoulder-level pan to the kiln; its temperature indicator flickers once, then stabilizes. The artist whispers, "That wasn't the timer." Hold on the closed kiln.

CAMERA: Start at 85mm macro, natural focus pull to the artist, then a slow motivated pan. No handheld shake.

PHYSICS: Real clay viscosity, wet slip on fingers, wheel inertia decelerates gradually, apron settles after the artist stops moving. Nothing emerges from the kiln.

AUDIO: Wheel motor, wet clay friction, room ventilation, one heavy internal metal knock, short natural whisper. No score, no sting.

CONTINUITY: Do not alter identity, hand count, bowl shape, furniture, kiln door, or light direction.
```

## 3. Fire Lookout — documentary weather sequence

**Mode:** Text-to-video · **Duration:** 12s · **Aspect ratio:** 16:9 · **Audio:** wind + radio

```text
Create a 12-second observational documentary clip at a remote mountain fire-lookout tower before a summer storm. A fictional middle-aged ranger with sun-weathered features, a red field jacket, binocular harness, and tied-back dark hair remains visually consistent.

0–4s — exterior wide shot: the ranger steps onto the narrow balcony as wind bends the pines below; layered thunderclouds advance across distant ridges. 4–8s — camera tracks beside the ranger as they raise binoculars; a pale dry-lightning flash illuminates the valley without showing flames. 8–12s — close three-quarter profile; the ranger presses a shoulder radio and calmly reports, "Dry strike, northeast ridge. Marking coordinates now." They look down to a paper map as the first large raindrops hit the railing.

CAMERA: Natural-history documentary realism, 50mm equivalent, restrained shoulder-mounted movement, no drone transition.

PHYSICS: Jacket and loose hair respond to gust direction; trees move with believable mass; cloud movement is slow; rain begins sparsely, not as a sudden wall.

AUDIO: Layered ridge wind, creaking timber tower, distant thunder after the flash, radio compression on the voice, first raindrops on metal. No music.

AVOID: active wildfire, panic, impossible cloud speed, changing weather direction, unsafe behavior, face morphing, subtitles, logos, watermark.
```

## 4. The Missing Key — miniature noir mystery

**Mode:** Text-to-video · **Duration:** 9s · **Aspect ratio:** 4:3 · **Audio:** noir foley

```text
Create a 9-second handcrafted miniature noir film in 4:3. The set is a dollhouse-scale private detective office built from paper, wood, thread, and painted metal. A tiny original fox detective wears a cream trench coat and green felt hat.

0–3s — tabletop-height dolly past venetian-blind shadows toward the detective studying an empty velvet key box. 3–6s — a paper envelope slides under the door by itself and stops against the fox's shoe; the detective's ears lift, then the hat brim tilts as they look down. 6–9s — macro insert: a gloved paw opens the envelope, revealing only a pressed blue flower; lightning briefly silhouettes a small figure outside the frosted window. Cut to black on the next thunderclap.

VISUAL STYLE: Premium stop-motion miniature, visible paper fibers, tiny hand-painted imperfections, controlled 12-fps character movement with smooth camera motion.

PHYSICS: Envelope friction and deceleration are believable; cloth and paper keep their miniature scale; no object changes material.

AUDIO: Rain on a tin roof, ticking desk clock, envelope scrape, distant thunder, one muted jazz bass note at the end.

AVOID: readable writing, branded props, famous characters, human hands entering frame, scale drift, plastic CGI surfaces, watermark.
```

## 5. A Single Bowl — historical market one-take

**Mode:** Text-to-video · **Duration:** 15s · **Aspect ratio:** 16:9 · **Audio:** crowd + foley, no dialogue

```text
Create a 15-second single-take historical street scene in a fictional river city inspired by late-medieval craft markets, without copying any specific culture or landmark.

SUBJECT: A young adult ceramic merchant in a moss-green linen tunic carries one blue-glazed bowl through a crowded morning market. Preserve their face, short curly hair, clothing, bowl color, and walking direction.

ACTION: The camera begins close on the blue bowl. It pulls back as the merchant carefully crosses the lane while porters, woven baskets, a handcart, and hanging fabrics create layered foreground motion. At 7 seconds, a running child nearly collides with the merchant; the merchant pivots, protects the bowl against their chest, and the handcart passes inches behind. They recover, laugh silently with relief, and place the intact bowl on a stall at 13 seconds. Hold on the bowl catching sunlight.

CAMERA: One continuous 35mm tracking shot, chest height, gentle lateral parallax, no hidden cuts or speed ramps.

PHYSICS: The bowl has convincing weight; clothes trail behind turns; crowd members avoid collisions; cart wheels maintain contact with uneven stones.

AUDIO: Morning crowd murmur, wood wheels, sandals, fabric flutter, birds, ceramic ring when the bowl touches the table. No modern sounds, dialogue, or music.

AVOID: famous architecture, fantasy elements, costume changes, duplicated people, broken anatomy, anachronistic objects, signs, text, watermark.
```
